## 2. MYTH CONTINUITY NOTE

### Verified Folk Traditions vs. Original Fictional Transformations

#### A. Verified Folk Traditions
* **Domus de Janas (Fairies' Houses)**:
  * *Classification*: `FOLK TRADITION` / `VERIFIED FACT` (Archaeological).
  * *Description*: Pre-Nuragic rock-cut chamber tombs found throughout Sardinia, carved into limestone, trachyte, or granite outcrops (dating primarily to Ozieri culture, c. 4000–3200 BCE).
  * *Folklore*: Popular Sardinian tradition attributes these chambers to *Janas*—small, supernatural weaver-women who live in stone houses, weave golden thread on looms, and possess foresighted or guardian powers.
  * *Evidence Source*: Sardegna Cultura (Regione Autonoma della Sardegna) – *Necropoli ipogeiche della Sardegna*.
* **S'Urtzu / Ancestral Winter Rites**:
  * *Classification*: `FOLK TRADITION`.
  * *Description*: Archaic horned figure in central Sardinian carnival rituals (notably Mamoiada/Ula Tirso), representing a sacrificial earth-creature tied to agrarian death and rebirth cycles.
  * *Evidence Source*: Museo delle Maschere Mediterranee (Mamoiada); Sardegna Cultura.

#### B. Original Fictional Transformations (`ORIGINAL_FICTIONAL_TRANSFORMATION`)
* **The Janas Resonance Lattice**: In this narrative, the *Domus de Janas* were constructed not merely as tombs, but as acoustic-piezoelectric nodes carved into specific trachyte/granite formations. When excited by subterranean infrasound (such as the 9 Hz fault pulse associated with *S'Urtzu*), the rock chambers act as stationary acoustic resonators that stabilize human cognitive/memory functions within a specific radius.
* **The Vector Alignment (108° ESE)**: The directional vector established at Grotta di Nettuno links coastal marine karst siphons to interior *Domus de Janas* nodes along a structural fault line crossing central Sardinia.
