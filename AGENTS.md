# SARDINIA MYTHS — ZERO-INVENTION RESEARCH FIREWALL

## Absolute rule

No writer, planner, inspector or autopilot process may invent or guess:

* Historical people
* Ships, manifests or voyages
* Dates or historical events
* Archaeological discoveries
* Artifact names, materials or excavation details
* Buildings, monuments or architectural properties
* Geographic routes, distances or coordinates
* Local traditions, festivals or rituals
* Sardinian words, dialects or translations
* Myth names, origins, behavior or regional associations
* Scientific, nautical or geological claims

If a factual claim cannot be verified online, it must be removed or explicitly classified as fictional. Never present plausible invention as history.

## Canon priority

Before researching or writing, load the recovered books and immutable canon.

Priority order:

1. Approved immutable saga canon
2. Recovered books
3. Previously locked chapters
4. Verified external sources
5. New fictional transformation

Later layers cannot silently contradict earlier layers.

## Mandatory two-pass online research

Before every chapter:

### Pass A — Writer research

The writer independently researches every planned:
* Location
* Route
* Building
* Historical person
* Myth
* Artifact
* Archaeological site
* Cultural tradition
* Nautical or scientific mechanism

### Pass B — Auditor research

A separate auditor repeats the research from scratch. It must not rely on the writer’s summaries or conclusions.

The auditor compares both research sets claim by claim. Disagreement automatically blocks drafting.

## Source hierarchy

Prefer:

1. Official archaeological parks and museums
2. Italian Ministry of Culture
3. Sardegna Cultura and Sardegna Turismo
4. Municipal and regional authorities
5. UNESCO
6. Universities and peer-reviewed research
7. Recognized historical archives
8. Reputable books and cultural institutions

Blogs may help locate a tradition but cannot independently certify disputed history. Wikipedia may assist discovery but cannot be the only source for important claims.

## Evidence requirements

Every factual claim requires:

* Exact claim
* Source title
* Direct URL
* Publisher or institution
* Access date
* Supporting passage or precise paraphrase
* Confidence: HIGH, MEDIUM or DISPUTED
* Classification: VERIFIED FACT, FOLK TRADITION, SCHOLARLY THEORY or ORIGINAL FICTION

Search-result snippets are not evidence. The underlying page must be opened and checked.

## Minimum-source rules

* Ordinary factual claim: one authoritative source
* Historical person or event: two independent credible sources
* Disputed archaeology: two sources plus explicit uncertainty
* Folklore variant: one Sardinian cultural source plus one corroborating source when available
* Exact route or nautical claim: current official navigation, port or operator source

## Fiction boundary

Original magic is permitted and required, but it must be labeled internally as `ORIGINAL_FICTIONAL_TRANSFORMATION`.

The story may creatively awaken a verified myth, but it cannot rewrite invented behavior as authentic folklore.

Example:

* “Cogas were associated with blood-drinking and animal transformation” → verify as folklore.
* “Cogas steal identity through marine-radio voices” → original fictional transformation.
* “Cogas follow base-seven mathematics” → fictional unless a direct source proves it.

These classifications belong in the research file, not as explanations inside the novel.

## Naming firewall

All named historical people, vessels, artifacts, documents and institutions must exist in the evidence ledger.

Any unsupported proper noun triggers:

`UNVERIFIED_ENTITY_FAIL`

The 1642 Sanctity manifest is prohibited unless an authoritative historical record is produced. Otherwise replace it with a clearly fictional unnamed wreck; never assign it a fake historical identity.

## Geography and physics firewall

Build every route using real geography:

`DEPARTURE → TRANSIT → INTERMEDIATE POSITION → ARRIVAL`

No unexplained jumps. No teleportation. No yacht entering deep-sea trenches or underwater caves. Supernatural perception may distort what characters see, but physical position, time, vessel capability and damage must remain consistent.

Grotta di Nettuno is a sea-level cave entrance beneath Capo Caccia—not a deep-sea destination.

## Independent inspections

The Historical Auditor, Myth Auditor, Geographic Auditor and Canon Auditor must:

* Search online independently
* Cite evidence
* Quote the manuscript claim being checked
* Return PASS, FAIL or DISPUTED
* Never inherit another inspector’s verdict
* Never hardcode PASS
* Never accept UI state as manuscript evidence

## Fail-closed behavior

If browsing fails, evidence is missing or sources conflict:

* Stop drafting
* Mark the claim `UNVERIFIED`
* Do not improvise
* Replace it with verified material or remove it
* Keep the chapter unlocked

## Locking requirements

A chapter locks only when:

* Actual manuscript exists
* Prose count is 3,800–5,500 words
* Canon comparison passes
* Every factual claim exists in the evidence ledger
* Every fictional transformation is labeled internally
* All 20 inspectors provide evidence-based results
* No unsupported names, dates, artifacts or locations remain
* Final manuscript and reports are saved

A successful application build does not certify a chapter.

# CHAPTER CONTINUITY, MYTH EVOLUTION & AMULET DIRECTIVE

## Core objective

The saga follows one continuous, believable expedition through Sardinia to recover exactly **12 true amulets**.

Real history, geography, buildings, archaeology, folklore, minerals and people must be verified. Only the supernatural awakening, magical powers, puzzles and fictional conflict may be created.

No thirteenth amulet. No teleportation. No unexplained travel. No repeated chapter structures.

---

## Before every chapter: Direction Guide

Create `00_chapter_direction_guide.md` before drafting.

### 1. Previous-chapter continuity

Analyze the complete previous chapter and record:

* Exact final location and time
* Every character’s physical position
* Injuries, exhaustion and emotional state
* Item and amulet custody
* Vehicle condition
* Missing memories
* Promises, secrets and disagreements
* Active myths
* Unresolved threats
* Physical damage and environmental consequences
* Final sentence and immediate next action

The next chapter begins from this state. No reset, invisible recovery or unexplained relocation.

### 2. Myth Continuity Note

For every previously awakened myth, record:

* Current location
* Current form
* Objective
* Power gained or lost
* Relationship with other myths
* Relationship with each team member
* Whether it follows, hides, transforms or withdraws
* Required return chapter
* Unresolved consequence

A myth does not disappear because its chapter ended. It may return much later, influence another myth remotely, occupy an artifact or leave a consequence that survives across several chapters.

### 3. New myth direction

Research the next myth online again, even if it was researched previously.

Verify:

* Authentic name and spelling
* Regional variations
* Towns or areas associated with it
* Traditional appearance
* Recorded behavior
* Cultural meaning
* Protection or ritual associated with it
* Disputed interpretations

Then create one `ORIGINAL_FICTIONAL_TRANSFORMATION` showing how an approaching or activated amulet awakens it.

Its manifestation must differ from every previous myth awakening.

### 4. Location intelligence dossier

Research the exact next location:

* Town and local names
* Geographic route
* Travel duration
* Archaeological sites
* Nuraghi, sacred wells and necropolises
* Churches, castles, towers and historic houses
* Caves and natural wonders
* UNESCO sites
* Spiritual or ritual places
* Genuine prehistoric artifacts
* Excavations and discoveries
* Historical events
* Famous people born there or connected to it
* Local legends
* Traditional occupations and crafts
* Food, music, clothing and festivals
* Present-day local life

Select only elements that actively affect the adventure. Never insert history as a lecture or tourism list.

### 5. Local human integration

Local people must feel authentic and live normal lives before magic reaches them.

They may be:

* Archaeologists
* Fishermen
* Shepherds
* Museum workers
* Artists
* Historians
* Guides
* Craftspeople
* Residents
* Religious custodians

Fictional local characters may exist, but must be clearly fictional. Never assign invented actions or quotations to real living people. Historical figures enter through verified work, documents, buildings, art, laws or legacy.

---

## Amulet architecture

Exactly 12 true amulets exist.

Every true amulet requires:

1. A verified Sardinian location
2. A real mineral or stone
3. A historically plausible physical material
4. A myth connected to its protection
5. A unique acquisition challenge
6. A unique activation condition
7. A useful survival power
8. A serious cost
9. A limitation
10. A combination effect with another amulet
11. A corruption or misuse risk
12. A verification method distinguishing it from a fake

### Mineral rule

Research every mineral before selection:

* Geological presence in Sardinia
* Historic mine or discovery location
* Physical properties
* Color and crystal structure
* Historical use
* Toxicity or handling risk

The mineral remains scientifically real. Its supernatural power is labeled fictional.

### Acquisition complexity

An amulet cannot be obtained by merely:

* Opening a box
* Finding a hidden room
* Defeating one guardian
* Solving one inscription
* Placing an object in a socket
* Receiving it from a helpful spirit

Recovery requires interconnected historical, geographical, mythological, emotional and mechanical challenges.

Some challenges extend across several chapters. The group may reach an amulet and still fail to obtain, activate or safely carry it.

---

## False-amulet system

Certain myths may create or offer convincing false amulets.

A false amulet may:

* Copy one visible power
* Alter the route
* Corrupt another amulet
* Replace a memory
* Turn one myth against another
* Attach itself to the wrong carrier
* Lead part of the team to a false destination
* Appear historically authentic while containing the wrong mineral

Every false amulet must have detectable evidence. The reader must later realize that the clues were present.

The dashboard must separately track:

* `TRUE_AMULETS_CONFIRMED`
* `TRUE_AMULETS_UNVERIFIED`
* `FALSE_AMULETS_DETECTED`
* `SUSPECTED_OBJECTS`

Never increase the true total beyond 12.

---

## Myth-crossing system

Myths may:

* Hunt one another
* Protect competing amulets
* Exchange powers
* Create temporary alliances
* Deceive one another
* Use humans as intermediaries
* Occupy the same building differently
* Trigger another myth remotely
* Awaken an older enemy
* Transform when exposed to an amulet’s mineral

Every crossing needs a logical cause and persistent consequence.

No myth exists only to provide a battle. Each has desires, laws, fears, memory and a relationship with Sardinia.

---

## Group-spirit rule

The group’s bond is central, but the six characters do not need to remain physically together constantly.

When they split:

* Give the separation a necessary strategic reason
* Track both timelines
* Record who carries every artifact
* Preserve communication limitations
* Give each subgroup a distinct objective
* Show emotional consequences
* Prevent impossible knowledge transfer
* Establish a realistic reunion route

Every member must contribute unique knowledge or courage. No character may become decorative.

Mia and Tina remain non-magical animals. Their natural senses and loyalty may matter, but they never glow, detect supernatural frequencies, solve magical puzzles or act as magical instruments.

## Adventure and pacing standard

Every chapter must contain:

* Immediate continuity from the previous ending
* A compelling opening disturbance
* A real location functioning inside the conflict
* A mythological development
* A difficult decision
* A physical or emotional risk
* A surprising reversal
* A meaningful group interaction
* A persistent consequence
* A powerful but non-repeated ending

Challenges must threaten more than death. Characters may risk:

* Losing an amulet
* Activating a false amulet
* Forgetting another team member
* Becoming separated
* Damaging the Sentina
* Awakening a stronger myth
* Losing the route
* Sacrificing an important memory
* Giving an enemy control of an amulet power

Never solve a major threat in the same scene in which it appears.

# PARALLEL TEAM, DOUBLE-RESEARCH & HERITAGE COVERAGE RULE

## Team movement

The group must alternate naturally between:

* Full-group chapters
* Temporary subgroups
* Parallel missions
* Individual emergency actions
* Strategic reunions

Example:

* Katia, Veerle and Inga investigate a verified mineral deposit, mine, museum collection or geological site.
* Geronimo, Maris and André confront the next awakened myth or protect the route.

This is an example, not a permanent gender division. Teams must change according to expertise, injuries, trust, artifact custody and story necessity.

## Parallel-timeline control

When the group splits, record:

* Exact departure point and time
* Members of each subgroup
* Individual objective
* Transportation method
* Communication capability
* Amulet and equipment custody
* Risks unique to each mission
* Synchronization deadline
* Physical reunion location

Alternate scenes without repeating events. Neither subgroup may know what the other discovered until communication or reunion occurs.

Both missions must affect each other. A mineral removed by one subgroup may weaken, transform or enrage the myth confronting the other.

## Mineral mission rules

Before using any mineral, verify twice online:

1. Geological or mineralogical authority
2. Museum, university, mining archive or regional cultural source

Confirm:

* Mineral truly occurs in Sardinia
* Exact mine or geological area
* Chemical composition
* Physical properties
* Historical extraction or use
* Toxicity and safe handling
* Whether public access is realistic

Never invent a Sardinian mine, mineral deposit or historical use.

## Double-source verification

Every historical, archaeological, geographic, folkloric and architectural claim requires two checks.

Use:

* Writer Research Pass
* Independent Auditor Research Pass

Important claims require two independent credible sources. At least one should be authoritative or institutional.

No source may merely copy the other. Search snippets, AI summaries and dashboard text are not evidence.

## Heritage coverage register

Maintain a master register for:

* UNESCO World Heritage properties
* Official tentative-list sites
* Nuraghi
* Domus de janas
* Sacred wells
* Giants’ tombs
* Phoenician, Punic and Roman sites
* Medieval castles and towers
* Historic churches
* Museums and archaeological collections
* Caves and natural monuments
* Historic mines
* Artist and writer heritage
* Local festivals and spiritual traditions

Track every item as:

`RESEARCHED` → `PLANNED` → `USED` → `CONSEQUENCE_ACTIVE` → `RESOLVED`

Never label a site UNESCO unless the official UNESCO record confirms its exact status. Distinguish:

* Inscribed World Heritage property
* Tentative List
* National or regional heritage
* Archaeological site without UNESCO designation

## No-skipping gate

Before approving the route, compare planned chapters against the complete Heritage and Myth Registers.

The system must flag:

* Important myth omitted
* Major historical building omitted
* Relevant UNESCO property omitted
* Repeated town or monument
* Myth used without its regional origin
* Heritage mentioned but not used in the plot
* Mineral assigned to the wrong region
* Famous person connected to the wrong town

Not every heritage site must appear in Book I, but every important candidate must be researched, assigned to the most suitable book or deliberately excluded with a recorded reason.

## Creative intersection

Parallel missions must eventually collide unexpectedly.

Example structure:

1. The mineral team removes a real crystal from an authenticated geological setting.
2. Its absence changes the awakened myth’s behavior elsewhere.
3. The myth-challenge team believes it has solved the confrontation.
4. The mineral activates inside the first subgroup’s equipment.
5. Both teams discover they completed different halves of the same amulet puzzle.
6. Reunion creates a new danger rather than an immediate victory.

The history remains real. The mineral remains real. The location remains real. Only the supernatural connection is invented and clearly classified.

## Final enforcement

A chapter involving split teams cannot lock unless:

* Both timelines are continuous
* Both routes are geographically possible
* Every factual claim passes double verification
* Artifact custody remains exact
* No subgroup gains impossible knowledge
* The myth and mineral missions affect each other
* UNESCO and heritage classifications are correct
* The reunion or continued separation is recorded for the next chapter

**The group’s unity is emotional and strategic—not permanent physical proximity.**

* Maris is Inga’s brother.
* Inga is Maris’s sister.
* Maris naturally calculates risks around Inga’s trusting behavior.

* André is Inga’s Colombian husband.
* Maris is André’s brother-in-law.
* André’s distrust and constant questions can create tension with Maris.
* Inga may defend André when the group misinterprets his questions as stupidity.
* André’s concealed history must unfold slowly; marriage alone does not prove or disprove his reliability.

## Geronimo and Maris

* Geronimo and Maris work together on ambitious projects for the future.
* Geronimo supplies programming, invention and optimistic vision.
* Maris supplies calculations, budgets, timelines, probability and operational control.
* Their friendship is strengthened by building projects together.
* Their natural conflict: Geronimo sees what could exist; Maris calculates what it will cost and how likely it is to fail.
* They understand one another’s working habits and can coordinate under pressure without long explanations.

## Barcelona company

* Geronimo and André jointly run a company in Barcelona.
* The company is the legitimate operational foundation behind their equipment, travel and the Sentina.
* The company’s exact name, industry, ownership percentages and history must not be invented until approved.
* Their business relationship creates practical trust, financial obligations and possible hidden tensions.
* André understands the Sentina because he is involved in the company’s operations—not because a new captain role appears without history.

## Geronimo’s profession

* Geronimo is a programmer.
* He thinks in systems, dependencies, patterns, failure states and hidden architecture.
* Programming shapes how he approaches puzzles but must not turn the prose into technical jargon.
* He cannot “hack” ancient artifacts.
* He may recognize recursive behavior, logical traps, corrupted patterns and interacting rules.
* His solutions must still respect physical history, mythology and the amulets’ established laws.

* `DOCUMENTED_FOLK_TRADITION`
* `DISPUTED_OR_VARIANT_TRADITION`
* `ORIGINAL_FICTIONAL_TRANSFORMATION`

Map the route without unexplained jumps or unnecessary backtracking. Every chapter assignment must include physical arrival and departure, historical integration, local people or living culture, a unique myth manifestation, and consequences that continue into later chapters.

# EXPAND MYTH ATLAS — MINIMUM 25 VERIFIED TRADITIONS

Replace the fixed fifteen-myth production list with an expandable, location-driven atlas containing at least 25 independently verified Sardinian mythic or ritual traditions.

Research 30–36 candidates before selecting the final route. Never invent a traditional creature merely to reach the target.

For every candidate, record:

* authentic local and Italian names;
* town, subregion and known geographic variants;
* earliest reliable written or ethnographic source available;
* oral-tradition sources and disagreements;
* whether it is a legendary being, folk belief, ritual mask, historical custom or modern interpretation;
* traditional appearance, behaviour, limits and moral function;
* relationship with real monuments, caves, landscapes or festivals;
* planned narrative role;
* unique awakening trigger;
* amulet or mineral interaction;
* possible alliances and oppositions;
* first appearance, continuation chapters and later return;
* factual tradition separated from original fictional transformation.

Every myth must act differently. Repeating pursuit, possession, riddles, counting traps, dream imprisonment, shape-changing or identity theft automatically fails the Non-Repetition Gate.

At least:

* 5 myths must potentially help the group;
* 5 must remain morally ambiguous;
* 5 must function as hunters, thieves or deceivers;
* 3 must guard places or historical truths;
* 3 must directly oppose another myth;
* 2 must offer counterfeit amulets or false mineral instructions;
* 1 must become a long-term ally;
* 1 apparent ally must conceal a dangerous private objective.

Helping myths may save individual members, guide separated teams, interrupt hostile myths or reveal forgotten routes. Assistance must always follow verified folkloric character or be clearly marked as an original fictional development.

Myths may cross chapters, use one another, bargain, deceive, combine powers or fight over an amulet. Crossovers require a cause, individual motives and lasting consequences.

A defeated myth does not automatically disappear. Record whether it is wounded, indebted, displaced, sleeping, transformed, allied, imprisoned or still hunting.

No chapter may be drafted until the Myth Atlas shows at least 25 approved traditions and the five-book route assigns every selected myth a first awakening, continuing consequence and later reappearance or formal closure.
