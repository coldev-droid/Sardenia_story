# AUDITOR RESEARCH LOG: BATCH 1 (NORTHWEST & NURRA)

## CLAIM-101: Nuraghe Palmavera
**Writer Claim:** Middle Bronze Age complex in Alghero consisting of two main towers and a village of approximately 150-200 huts.
**Auditor Findings:**
- **Verification:** PASS
- **Source:** sardegnacultura.it, sardegnaversounesco.org
- **Details:** The core consists of two central towers (one limestone from 15-14th c. BCE, one sandstone from 9th c. BCE). The village originally contained 150-200 circular huts, though only around 50 are visible today.
- **Classification:** VERIFIED_FACT

## CLAIM-102: Necropolis of Anghelu Ruju
**Writer Claim:** Contains 38 'domus de janas' (rock-cut tombs) featuring architectural details like false doors and was used from the Late Neolithic through the early Bronze Age.
**Auditor Findings:**
- **Verification:** PASS
- **Source:** sardegnacultura.it, algheroexperience.it
- **Details:** Confirmed 38 underground burial chambers carved into sandstone. Dates back to Late Neolithic/Early Copper Age (3300-2700 BC) up to Bronze Age. False doors (false porte) are a prominent symbolic feature representing passage to the afterlife.
- **Classification:** VERIFIED_FACT

## CLAIM-103: Sa Mama 'e su Sole
**Writer Claim:** Mythical figure in Sardinian folklore who appears during the hottest hours of the day and leaves a fever mark on children who venture outside.
**Auditor Findings:**
- **Verification:** PASS
- **Source:** distrettoculturaledelnuorese.it, unionesarda.it
- **Details:** Confirmed as "The Mother of the Sun." She appears after midday, catches disobedient children playing in the heat, and touches their forehead leaving a mark that causes a fever known as "halentura". Used as a cautionary tale to prevent heatstroke.
- **Classification:** FOLK_TRADITION

## CLAIM-104: Grotta di Nettuno
**Writer Claim:** Sea-level cave located beneath Capo Caccia in Alghero, reachable by sea or via the Escala del Cabirol.
**Auditor Findings:**
- **Verification:** PASS
- **Source:** grottadinettuno.it, frommers.com
- **Details:** Confirmed stalactite cave at the base of Capo Caccia cliffs. Accessible by boat (sea level entrance) or by land via the "Escala del Cabirol" (a staircase of 654 steps cut into the cliff face).
- **Classification:** VERIFIED_FACT

## AUDIT CONCLUSION
- All 4 claims independently verified.
- No historical invention detected.
- Sources are authoritative and corroborate the writer's findings.
- **BATCH 1 STATUS: CLEARED FOR INTEGRATION**
