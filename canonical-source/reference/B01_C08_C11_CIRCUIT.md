# Book I — Chapters 8–11 Story Circuit Arc

## Arc purpose: the first amulet changes the journey

Chapters 5–7 end with the Janas Key accepting shared custody at Capo Caccia because the group chose rescue over possession. This arc refuses the easy next move. They must recover, protect their local helpers, travel through real November weather, and learn that their first amulet has made them visible. By Chapter 11 they reach the Sinis threshold with a route that feels earned rather than jumped.

| Chapter | Time / route | Primary engine | End-state that creates the next chapter |
|---|---|---|---|
| 8 — *The Photograph at the Harbour Wall* | Morning after rescue; Capo Caccia → Alghero | Public consequence / protection of others | Their rescue image is online; an Alghero contact is placed at risk, so they must leave discreetly. |
| 9 — *The Iron Road South* | November rain; Alghero → Sassari → Macomer → Oristano | Transit realism / contained trust test | A delayed rail transfer exposes an actual tail and gives the group the first verified evidence of the father’s network. |
| 10 — *The Law of the Sparrowhawk* | Oristano and Arborense plain | Historical investigation / civic moral trap | Eleonora d’Arborea’s legal legacy provides the custody model for the Key; the route points to Cabras. |
| 11 — *The Mural of the First Witness* | San Sperate detour → Cabras | Art, testimony and revelation | A mural’s archive detail identifies the Salt Compass; the group reaches Cabras with a warning about Tharros. |

## Chapter 8 — The Photograph at the Harbour Wall

| Required component | Detail |
|---|---|
| Previous ending | Begins moments after Chapter 7: soaked rope, rescue personnel, dogs wrapped in emergency blankets, the Janas Key warm inside its shared case. |
| Place / building | Capo Caccia service point → Alghero Catalan bastions and harbour wall; the city is seen in pale post-storm light, not as a tourist montage. |
| Weather / season | November mistral easing; salt spray, sun breaking through broken cloud, wet limestone still dangerous. |
| Transport | Coast guard/official shuttle down from the incident; local licensed taxi back to Alghero; no heroic off-road shortcut. |
| History / folklore | Alghero’s Catalan-Sardinian identity is heard in a greeting and on signage. A fisher recalls a non-supernatural sea warning used by older crews. |
| Amulet / mineral | Janas Key: bronze, rock crystal, red copper thread. It refracts light into three paths when the group quarrels, showing only that choice has consequences. |
| Disaster / turn | A news photograph shows Geronimo holding rope during the rescue. The image also captures a charcoal-coated figure on the cliff behind him—visible to the reader only after Veerle enlarges it. |
| Emotional shift | Geronimo realizes even doing the right thing can endanger helpers. He offers to leave alone; the group rejects the false nobility of that idea. |
| End handoff | Maris confirms the next safe move is scheduled rail south through Sassari and Macomer, using ordinary tickets and changing no one’s name. |

## Chapter 9 — The Iron Road South

| Required component | Detail |
|---|---|
| Opening flow | Starts with packing and dog care, not an instant chase. The case is placed in shared custody; everyone receives a key number, nobody receives sole authority. |
| Route | Alghero → Sassari by regional service/bus connection; Sassari → Macomer → Oristano by the Sardinian rail backbone, with realistic waits and rain disruption. |
| Place / building | Sassari station; Macomer junction on the Marghine plateau; the physical station clocks, ticket validation, wet platforms and limited food options matter. |
| Historical layer | Railway is not magical. Its historical importance is connection across an island of hard terrain. A station display references the island’s rail history without claiming perfect old continuity. |
| Folklore / myth | **S’Ammutadore**, the night-pressure figure, appears only as a tale an older passenger tells about waking unable to move. The story primes a dream but does not create a literal demon. |
| Amulet / mineral | Rock crystal fogs from the inside when a lie enters the group’s conversation; it does not identify the liar. |
| Disaster / turn | Flooded track section causes a long halt near Macomer. A passenger’s abandoned bag is mistakenly treated as a threat; this teaches the group not to turn fear into accusation. The real evidence is a copied ticket record proving someone followed their exact route. |
| Character turn | André tells the group he once lied by omission about the childhood port object. He submits his phone and notes to shared review, earning a first small measure of trust. |
| End handoff | Arriving late in Oristano, they receive a message from a civil archivist: the symbol on the Key resembles a custody mark connected to the **Carta de Logu**. |

## Chapter 10 — The Law of the Sparrowhawk

| Required component | Detail |
|---|---|
| Place / building | Oristano historic centre, archive/municipal reading room, and Piazza Eleonora; wet basalt streets, shutters, lamps, courthouse quiet. |
| Historical person | **Eleonora d’Arborea** and the **Carta de Logu**: historic legal code, discussed accurately as an important medieval Sardinian legal tradition. Her real legacy is not made into a ghost or a spell. |
| Historical detail that changes plot | A clause/model of guardianship and responsibility gives Veerle language to formulate shared custody: record every movement, no private sale, no private opening, witness every transfer. |
| Folklore / legend | **Sa Filonzana** returns as an old metaphor for a thread of consequences. A local researcher contrasts fate stories with law: a thread may be imagined, but people remain responsible for the cut they make. |
| Amulet / mineral | The Janas Key’s copper filament straightens when the group signs a plain-language custody record. Magic confirms consensus only after human accountability exists. |
| Weather / season | Hard rain prevents an evening route to Cabras; the overnight stay matters. |
| Disaster / turn | The father’s network attempts to purchase the Key through a legal-looking intermediary. The offer includes evidence about Geronimo’s mother. The group refuses both payment and private information. |
| Emotional turn | Geronimo chooses not to open the evidence alone. This is the first direct break from the father’s cycle of secrecy. |
| End handoff | The archive index names a Cabras family collection and a coastal survey at Tharros. The group schedules transport for morning, with a local appointment rather than an unplanned site entry. |

## Chapter 11 — The Mural of the First Witness

| Required component | Detail |
|---|---|
| Opening flow | Rain clears. A deliberate detour through **San Sperate** is justified by a mural conservator who has a copied visual record connected to the Cabras collection; it is not a random sightseeing stop. |
| Place / building | San Sperate’s *Paese Museo* streets and mural walls; later the road to Cabras and the lagoon landscape. |
| Historical person | **Pinuccio Sciola** is treated as the real artist who helped make San Sperate a mural village; no invented personal dialogue or magical attribution. His work establishes the theme that stone can hold human memory through art. |
| Myth / folklore | Giants and guardian imagery appear in painted forms; the characters distinguish folk “giants” stories from the archaeological **Giants of Mont’e Prama**. |
| Amulet / mineral | The Key’s rock crystal catches a pigment colour that matches a notation on the copied coastal survey. It points toward the future **Salt Compass**: weathered bronze, halite, azurite—still not found. |
| Transport | Hired van / bus connection, with luggage, dog breaks and route time shown; Cabras is reached late afternoon, not instantly. |
| Disaster / turn | A mural panel is vandalized overnight with the father’s black circle. It is not magic: paint analysis and a witness show a human operative. The group protects the conservator from public blame. |
| Character turn | Inga becomes the first witness who records fact before fear. Her report separates what was seen, what is inferred, and what the Eye showed. |
| End handoff | At Cabras lagoon, the Key projects a compass needle toward Tharros only at low tide. A local warns that the coastal path will be closed by wind the next day. Book II’s sea-memory journey begins under a real weather deadline. |

## Continuity ledger after Chapter 11

- **Custody:** Janas Key held in a witnessed shared case; movement log begun under the Carta de Logu-inspired agreement.
- **Route:** Cabras → Tharros/Sinis at the next safe tide and legal access window.
- **Antagonist:** A human network can photograph, follow and vandalize; the father’s influence is not an excuse to accuse strangers.
- **Characters:** Geronimo no longer keeps revelations private; André has begun evidence-based repair; Inga owns the factual record; Katia and Maris lead safety/logistics; Veerle safeguards interpretation.
- **Myth handling:** Janas, Filonzana and giants are culturally grounded narrative lenses; their physical sites and historic records remain distinct.

## Anti-repetition check

The four chapters progress through public aftermath, rail transit, legal-history investigation and art/testimony. None repeats cave horror, ferry danger, a mirror anomaly, or a simple villain fight.
