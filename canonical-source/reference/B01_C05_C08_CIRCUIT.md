# Book I — Chapters 5–8 Story Circuit Arc

## Arc purpose: Earning the Janas Key and the Cost of Visibility

Following the refusal of the Obsidian Eye's false route in Chapter 4, the group travels to the lawful archaeological site of Anghelu Ruju. This arc moves them from quiet investigation into public, physical danger on the cliffs of Capo Caccia. The core philosophical test of the series is resolved here: when forced to choose between claiming an amulet and saving a human life, the group chooses the rescue. This earns them the Janas Key but destroys their anonymity, perfectly setting up their flight via the railway in Chapter 9.

| Chapter | Time / route | Primary engine | End-state that creates the next chapter |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5 — *The Red Ochre Boundary* | Morning; Alghero → Anghelu Ruju | Archaeological investigation / crowd evasion | The Eye aligns the tombs' red ochre with Capo Caccia; a Sentinel spotter forces a rapid relocation. |
| 6 — *The Roe Deer's Steps* | Afternoon; Anghelu Ruju → Capo Caccia | Physical endurance / environmental hazard | The group descends the 654 cliff steps (Escala del Cabirol) in rising Mistral winds; the Janas Key is located, but a local fisher is stranded by the tide. |
| 7 — *The Weight of the Rope* | Dusk; Base of Capo Caccia cliffs | Ethical choice / tactical rescue | Geronimo abandons the Key to hold a rescue line. The Key accepts shared custody. The Coast Guard arrives, ensuring survival but guaranteeing exposure. |
| 8 — *The Photograph at the Harbour Wall* | Morning after rescue; Capo Caccia → Alghero | Public consequence / protection of others | Their rescue image is online; an Alghero contact is placed at risk, so they must leave discreetly. |

## Chapter 5 — The Red Ochre Boundary

| Required component | Detail |
|---|---|
| Previous ending | Begins the morning after the storm in Alghero. The group has rejected the Eye's illusion and is driving to the Anghelu Ruju necropolis as ordinary tourists. |
| Place / building | Anghelu Ruju necropolis. The flat, limestone plain dotted with rock-cut tombs. |
| Weather / season | Early November morning. Crisp, cold sunlight. The earth is still soaked and smells of wet thyme and limestone. |
| Transport | The hired, pet-friendly van from Chapter 4. Driven legally and parked in the public visitor lot. |
| History / folklore | **Domus de Janas**: The group examines the authentic carvings of bull horns and traces of red ochre (symbolizing blood and rebirth). They respect the boundary ropes, acting as scholars rather than looters. |
| Amulet / mineral | The Obsidian Eye acts purely as a lens. When held up to the entrance of a specific tomb, it highlights how the ancient architects aligned the doorway perfectly with the setting sun over the distant cliffs of Capo Caccia. |
| Disaster / turn | A Sentinel operative is spotted embedded in a German tour group. Maris identifies his concealed comms. |
| Emotional shift | The transition from safety to hunted. They cannot fight in a public archaeological park. They must use crowd dynamics to slip away unnoticed. |
| End handoff | They reach the van and must immediately drive toward the coastal promontory of Capo Caccia to beat the Father's network to the location. |

## Chapter 6 — The Roe Deer's Steps

| Required component | Detail |
|---|---|
| Place / building | Capo Caccia promontory and the *Escala del Cabirol* (The Roe Deer's Steps)—a massive 654-step staircase carved directly into the sheer limestone cliff face, leading down to Neptune's Grotto. |
| Weather / season | Late afternoon. The Mistral wind is rising sharply. Freezing salt spray blasts the cliff face. The sea below is churning white. |
| History / folklore | The sheer drop of the Nurra coast. The historical reality of the cliffs acting as a brutal natural barrier and watchpoint for centuries. |
| Amulet / mineral | First sighting of the **Janas Key**: bronze, rock crystal, red copper thread. It is lodged in a high-tide fissure near the base of the stairs, perfectly camouflaged. |
| Disaster / turn | As the group attempts to navigate the treacherous lower stairs to secure the artifact, a rogue wave sweeps a local rock-fisher off a lower ledge. He is caught in a current, slamming against the limestone. |
| Emotional turn | The temptation of the artifact versus the immediate reality of a dying man. The group's paramillitary and scholarly focuses are instantly overridden by human empathy. |
| End handoff | Geronimo and André deploy their climbing rope, bracing against the slick rock, committing entirely to the rescue as the light begins to fail. |

## Chapter 7 — The Weight of the Rope

| Required component | Detail |
|---|---|
| Opening flow | Immediate continuation. A brutal physical struggle against the sea, wind, and the weight of a drowning man. |
| Place / building | The lowest, most dangerous ledges of Capo Caccia. |
| Transport | None. They are entirely reliant on physical endurance, friction, and their climbing rope. |
| History / folklore | Rejection of the "chosen one" myth. Survival depends on communal effort (holding the rope), mirroring traditional Sardinian societal bonds of mutual aid (*la paradura*). |
| Amulet / mineral | Geronimo drops his reach for the Janas Key to use both hands to brace the rope. Because it was not seized with greed, a subsequent wave washes the Key directly into a safe tidal pool at Katia's feet. It binds to the group's shared consent. |
| Disaster / turn | The physical toll is immense (rope burns, near-hypothermia). Local Coast Guard and emergency services arrive by rigid-inflatable boat (RIB). |
| Character turn | The cell solidifies. They save the man, but realize they are now trapped in a bureaucratic and highly visible rescue operation. |
| End handoff | Soaked rope, rescue personnel, dogs wrapped in emergency blankets. The Janas Key is warm inside its shared case. They are taken back up to the service point. |

## Chapter 8 — The Photograph at the Harbour Wall

| Required component | Detail |
|---|---|
| Previous ending | Begins moments after Chapter 7: soaked rope, rescue personnel, dogs wrapped in emergency blankets, the Janas Key warm inside its shared case. |
| Place / building | Capo Caccia service point → Alghero Catalan bastions and harbour wall; the city is seen in pale post-storm light, not as a tourist montage. |
| Weather / season | November mistral easing; salt spray, sun breaking through broken cloud, wet limestone still dangerous. |
| Transport | Coast guard/official shuttle down from the incident; local licensed taxi back to Alghero; no heroic off-road shortcut. |
| History / folklore | Alghero’s Catalan-Sardinian identity is heard in a greeting and on signage. A fisher recalls a non-supernatural sea warning used by older crews. |
| Amulet / mineral | Janas Key: bronze, rock crystal, red copper thread. It refracts light into three paths when the group quarrels, showing only that choice has consequences. |
| Disaster / turn | A news photograph shows Geronimo holding rope during the rescue. The image also captures a charcoal-coated figure on the cliff behind him—visible to the reader only after Veerle enlarges it. |
| Emotional shift | Geronimo realizes even doing the right thing can endanger helpers. He offers to leave alone; the group rejects the false nobility of that idea. |
| End handoff | Maris confirms the next safe move is scheduled rail south through Sassari and Macomer, using ordinary tickets and changing no one’s name. |
