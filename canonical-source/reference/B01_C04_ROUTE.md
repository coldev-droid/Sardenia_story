# Book I, Chapter 4 — Story Circuit

## Title
**The Road of the Janas**

## Purpose in the five-book saga
This is not an arrival chapter. It converts the sea-crossing mystery into a real Sardinian ground route. The group reaches the north-west coast after the altered crossing, discovers that the book’s “Nora” signal is not an instruction to sail blindly south, and earns the first reliable route method: evidence must be read with people and place, never alone.

## Continuity carried forward
* **Obsidian Eye awakened in Barcelona:** Remains sealed in Katia’s waterproof case; it can mark routes but cannot command transport.
* **Group consent pact:** The team refuses to follow the ferry diversion literally; the decision to land is collective.
* **André’s childhood memory:** A white harbour building and a tower trigger his fragmented recognition, but he cannot identify the place alone.
* **Nora written by the Echo:** Reframed as a future destination/route key, not an excuse to override maritime safety.
* **Father’s pursuit:** A real human contact at the harbour is suspected of reporting movements; suspicion must be verified, not assumed.
* **Dogs:** Mia and Tina are tired, cold and distressed after the crossing; their care dictates pace, taxi choice and lodging.

## Route card
* **Date / season:** Early November, dawn after an autumn storm
* **Start:** Ferry approach to Porto Torres / Gulf of Asinara
* **Destination:** Porto Torres → Sassari → Alghero, by legal ferry landing, pre-booked pet-friendly taxi/van, then a small licensed guesthouse
* **Weather:** Storm easing; hard north-west wind, standing water, rough sea, delayed road traffic
* **Practical constraint:** Dogs need water and calm; Katia’s wrist is aggravated; no rental car is immediately available; mobile signal is intermittent
* **Chapter engine:** Arrival under pressure, logistical investigation, and a land-based route choice—not a chase
* **Disaster:** The book’s map causes a false route projection toward an unsafe flooded access road; following it would trap the group
* **Exit state:** They reach Alghero safely, obtain a local historical lead, and learn the next lawful site: Anghelu Ruju

## Historical and geographic core
* **Porto Torres:** Roman Turris Libisonis and the port’s real layered identity establish that Sardinia begins with human trade, departure and arrival—not mystical emptiness.
* **Basilica of San Gavino:** It appears as a landmark and historical anchor: a long, low Romanesque basilica whose presence offers a physical route reference, not a supernatural temple.
* **Asinara / Gulf context:** The coast, wind and protected landscape are felt from the ferry; no illegal detour or invented access occurs.
* **Sassari–Alghero road:** Real travel time, rain, rest stops, dog needs and driver knowledge shape the chapter.
* **Alghero:** Catalan-Sardinian language, sea walls and wet stone create the setting for the local source who corrects the book’s false certainty.

## Historical person, folklore and legend layer
* **Sardus Pater:** Mentioned by a local historian as a layered ancient founder tradition, not fact used to validate the map. The Eye tries to turn it into a “chosen bloodline” claim; Veerle rejects that lie.
* **Is Janas:** A grandmother tells a child that the rock-cut tombs are fairy houses. The group later understands why people gave ancient rooms such names: they preserve mystery and care for the dead. No living Janas creature appears.
* **Domus de Janas:** The book uses the phrase as an ethical clue: a house of the dead is not a vault to loot. This establishes Anghelu Ruju as the next route node.

## Amulet and mineral logic
* **Active object:** Obsidian Eye: black volcanic glass set inside the damaged book spine; it magnifies attention and grief, not power.
* **Mineral / material:** Obsidian: sharp, reflective, historically traded stone; used here as a narrative metaphor for dangerous clarity, not as invented technology.
* **Future amulet seeded:** The Janas Key: a small rock-crystal and bronze key, not acquired in Chapter 4. Its first mark appears as a six-pointed scratch beside Anghelu Ruju.
* **Cost:** When Geronimo looks into the Eye’s black reflection, he hears his mother’s humming but loses his sense of direction for several minutes. He must let Katia and Maris guide him.

## Sensory mandate
* **Hear:** ferry chains, gulls fighting wind, multilingual terminal announcements, dogs’ claws on wet floor, taxi wipers, rain on Alghero stone.
* **Smell:** diesel, salt, wet wool, myrtle carried inland, coffee, old paper, rosemary in the guesthouse courtyard.
* **Feel:** nausea after the crossing, cramped muscles, Katia’s painful wrist, cold fingers warming around a cup, relief made fragile by the Eye.
* **See:** grey sea breaking white, basilica stone after rain, Alghero’s walls, yellow window light, black obsidian holding no ordinary reflection.

## Chapter end line requirement
The final beat is not a cliffhanger monster attack. It is Geronimo realizing the most seductive apparition in the world can still lead him into a real ditch—and choosing, for the first time, to trust the living hands pulling him back.
