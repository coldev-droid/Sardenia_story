# The Lock Crown of Sardinia — Final Saga Edition

## Production decision

This is the protected restart of the five-book saga. It preserves every recovered file, visual, route, research source and prior manuscript as an **archive layer**. It does not overwrite the certified Book I or falsely claim that old drafts are the new final edition.

The new edition uses a fresh continuity contract: Barcelona begins the story; Sardinia becomes the living labyrinth; the whole quest lasts **several months**, not three weeks; and the five books form one escalating journey for all twelve amulets.

## Series promise

An ordinary family of friends is pulled into a race across Sardinia after a mysterious old bookseller gives them a damaged book that remembers them. Twelve amulets are scattered through caves, sacred wells, coastlines, ruins, towns and mountain routes. Each one carries an **Echo**: not a superpower, but a fragment of a former life and an unfinished choice.

The group must survive physical danger, hostile weather, criminal pursuit, hidden history, distrust and supernatural encounters that become more intimate as the amulets reunite. The greatest danger is not a monster. It is the temptation to use the map at the end to make the dead return exactly as they were.

## Scale, word-count and route standard

- **Five books / 150 chapters / 30 chapters per book.**
- **Target length: 825,000–900,000 prose words** across the complete saga; working target approximately **850,000 words**.
- **Chapter target: 5,200–6,000 prose words.** No chapter below 4,000 words unless it is an intentional interlude approved in the chapter contract.
- The journey covers **several months**, with an explicit calendar. Travel, recovery days, research, weather holds, port waits and festival dates all occupy real time.
- Every chapter must earn one meaningful Sardinian element: a place, landscape, route, living tradition, historic layer, craft, food, language context, archaeological fact or local relationship. It must change the story; it cannot be a brochure paragraph.
- Every book must cross coast, interior and community life. The five-book route will cover the island’s major historical, mythic, geological and cultural regions without forcing every site into the plot.
- Before drafting, each chapter receives a route card: date/season, origin and destination, legal access, transport time, weather risk, local source, active amulet, character condition, action objective and emotional consequence.

## Chapter variation and momentum protocol

- No two adjacent chapters may use the same primary dramatic engine, location type, threat shape, discovery method or emotional outcome.
- Each chapter must be built around a distinct engine—such as pursuit, survival, investigation, sea passage, social pressure, moral trap, festival, legal/historical discovery, relationship rupture, recovery under threat, or a contained supernatural encounter—and the same engine may not recur unchanged later in the book.
- Every chapter requires a turn, a complication and an irreversible consequence. A disaster must grow from established weather, terrain, human decision, history or the amulet’s cost; it cannot be random spectacle.
- Quiet chapters are not filler: they must deepen a bond, expose a lie, establish a practical constraint, reveal a new route truth or set a disaster in motion.
- The end of each chapter changes what the group can safely believe, do, carry, trust or reach next.

## The new magic: Echoes, not spells

1. Every amulet holds an Echo of a person who previously carried the same responsibility.
2. An Echo can appear through dreams, reflections, sound, water, stone, old writing, or another living person under emotional pressure.
3. Echoes never give free answers. They reveal a choice, a consequence, or a memory that must be interpreted.
4. The price is personal: an amulet can intensify fear, grief, doubt or divided loyalty. It cannot be used safely by a person who wants control.
5. The twelve amulets do not form a treasure map. They form the **Map of Returns**—a living route to the buried place where every former guardian chose either possession or release.
6. No portal travel, easy resurrection, instant healing, invincible characters, or videogame-style magic exists. Travel, injuries, boats, permits, weather, food, money, fatigue and trust remain real.

## The family secret

The old bookseller in Barcelona is not introduced as a villain or a ghost. She is warm, unsettling, observant, and appears only when the group has reached a moral crossroads.

Later, the group discovers that she carries an Echo of their mother, who died young. Their father is also not simply “back from the dead”: across centuries his soul has returned in different lives and repeatedly hunted the twelve amulets, believing that the Map of Returns can repair his family. Each return has made the original love more distorted and dangerous.

The final revelation is not that the parents can be restored. It is that love without consent becomes possession. The group must choose whether to repeat the cycle or end it.

## Core cast engine

- **Geronimo:** the emotional centre; brave under pressure but vulnerable to the promise of getting family back.
- **Katia:** botanical and material-world intelligence; careful, grounded and physically limited after her established injury. She identifies what living landscapes demand rather than treating them as scenery.
- **Veerle:** pattern reader and moral challenger; refuses easy explanations and recognizes when an Echo tries to manipulate grief.
- **Maris:** logistics, risk and truth under pressure; makes the group survive the real world—routes, money, boats, timing, supplies and consequences.
- **Inga:** independent witness and decision-maker; carries the emotional cost of damaged trust and refuses group loyalty without accountability.
- **André:** restricted by prior trust damage. His arc is earned through action, not a sudden redemption speech.
- **Mia and Tina:** ordinary dogs. Their welfare, fear and needs keep every travel sequence human and real.

## Tone and genre rules

- Fantasy becomes physical adventure: cliff paths, caves, storms, sea passages, old cities, sacred spaces, mines, forts, darkness and exhaustion.
- Horror is intimate: a familiar voice in the wrong room, a memory from another century, water that reflects a life not yet lived, a friend speaking a sentence they could not know.
- Action has consequence: injuries, lost gear, weather holds, missed ferries, damaged trust and limited supplies change later chapters.
- Sardinia is never a generic occult landscape. Archaeology, religion, folklore and living communities remain distinct and treated with respect.
- The prose will be original: expansive, mythic, cinematic and emotionally precise, with ancient weight, fellowship, danger and wonder. It will not imitate any named author’s voice.
- Magic and adrenaline rise book by book, but every supernatural event has a cost, every action sequence has a tactical reality, and every victory creates a harder moral choice.

## Non-negotiable protection

1. Archive Edition materials remain preserved and traceable.
2. Final Saga Edition prose is created chapter by chapter, with a chapter contract and source/continuity review before it becomes approved.
3. Every new chapter records: route, date/season, amulet state, character condition, supernatural event, local cultural source, and end-state.
4. No chapter is silently replaced. Earlier versions remain recoverable.
5. The final ending rejects single-person sovereignty: the secret cannot belong to one family, one nation, one hero or one returned soul.

## The Swarm's Epic Mandate (Adrenaline & Discovery)

- **The Epic Voice:** The prose must be mythic, cinematic, and emotionally intense. It will carry the weight and scale of high fantasy (evoking the grandeur of *Lord of the Rings* or McKenzie-style grit), but it must remain fiercely original. Do not imitate; innovate.
- **Sardinia as a Labyrinth:** The island must be revealed organically through danger, history, myth, geology, and local craft. It must inspire awe and discovery, serving as a powerful promotional showcase of Sardinia's true depth, but it must **never** read like a tourist brochure. Every location is a tactical reality or a supernatural crucible.
- **Relentless Momentum:** Magic, horror, and adrenaline must escalate continuously. There are no "boring parts." Every chapter features unexpected turns, immediate threats, or devastating revelations.
- **The Swarm's Rigor:** The inspector swarm and auditor must rigorously enforce creativity, rejecting clichés and demanding complex, deeply woven adventure mechanics where every action has a real, bleeding consequence.

## First scene directive — Book I, Chapter 1

Barcelona. Late afternoon. A narrow old bookshop survives between renovated storefronts. Geronimo enters looking for a book that should not exist. The old woman behind the counter knows an exact childhood detail that no stranger should know, but she does not explain it. She gives him a damaged Sardinian volume with a pressed black-stone mark hidden in its spine.

Outside, the city continues normally. Inside the book, a handwritten route begins in Barcelona and ends at a place in Sardinia that has been crossed out again and again in different ink. The first time Geronimo touches the mark, he hears—not a voice—but his mother humming a melody he has not heard since he was a child.

**Chapter 1 ends before any answer is given.**
