# BOOK I — CHAPTER 3

### The Karst Echoes

The dawn over the Rada di Alghero did not warm the water; it merely turned the sea from obsidian to the dull grey of wet lead. To the west, the massive limestone headland of Capo Caccia rose nearly two hundred meters out of the Mediterranean, its vertical white cliffs sheared flat by millennia of salt wind and winter swells. Between the headland and the open sea, the perforated islet of Isola Foradada sat like a hollow tooth, its sea-arch swallowing the low morning tide with a hollow, rhythmic roar.

On the bridge of the research vessel, the engine throttles sat pulled back to dead slow. The twin diesel engines hummed beneath the deck plating, vibrating through the soles of Geronimo’s boots. 

"Depth under the keel is seventy-two meters," Veerle said without looking up from her console. Her fingers moved methodically across the multi-beam sonar interface. "The slope drops sharply off the eastern face of Capo Caccia. We’re holding three cable lengths off the cliff base. Wind is northwest at eight knots, swell under one meter. If we're going to launch, conditions won't get better than this."

Geronimo turned to the chart table, where Katia was overlaying topographic maps of the karst cave systems onto high-resolution bathymetric surveys. 

"We split here," Geronimo said, his voice quiet. "The main vessel cannot enter the shallow apron beneath the cliff face without risking the sonar array against submerged limestone shelves. Maris, you take the helm."

Maris stepped forward, taking the leather-wrapped wheel with a nod. Beside her, André adjusted his VHF headset, checking the cross-band relay settings, while Inga secured the secondary tie-downs on the main deck.

"We take the yacht into Porto di Alghero," Maris said, reviewing the port layout on her monitor. "Clear customs at the commercial quay, secure the slip beneath the Bastioni Magellano, and establish a permanent radio bridge on Channel 68 and the encrypted UHF relay. If the weather turns or the swell lifts against Capo Caccia, you’ll have less than twenty minutes before the sea entrance to the grotto closes up with white water."

"Understood," Geronimo said. "Keep the main engines idling once you dock. If we lose telemetry inside the karst, we drop back to ultra-low-frequency acoustic pings."

"Team B is ready," Inga called out from the companionway, holding a pair of heavy dry-bags. "The tender is fueled, the auxiliary battery packs are fully charged, and the hydrophone array is stowed in the stern locker."

Team A—Geronimo, Katia, and Veerle—descended the starboard ladder to the lower launch deck. The *Bisso*, a seven-meter reinforced aluminum utility launch fitted with a silent electric drive and deep-keel acoustic sensors, bobbed in the lee of the larger vessel’s hull. They loaded their equipment in silence: waterproof cases containing high-frequency signal analyzers, directional hydrophones, laser scanner units, and field lanterns.

With a soft mechanical click, the davit line released. The *Bisso* hit the water, settling into the clear, cold sea.

"Clear of the hull," André’s voice crackled over the short-range radio from the bridge above. "Good luck, Team A. We are under way for Alghero harbor."

The main vessel’s twin screws churned the blue-green water, turning her bow east toward the distant red-tiled roofs and stone ramparts of Alghero’s medieval sea walls. Geronimo engaged the *Bisso*’s electric motor. The launch glided westward, cutting across the glass-smooth shadow cast by the towering precipice of Capo Caccia.

***

In the sound of the electric motor’s low whine, Capo Caccia loomed higher until it blocked out the eastern sunrise entirely. The cliff face was a sheer wall of Jurassic limestone, pitted by salt-weathering, its narrow ledges inhabited only by nesting colonies of Eleonora’s falcons and yellow-legged gulls whose cries echoed sharply against the stone.

"Look at the water clarity," Katia said, leaning over the gunwale. Twenty meters below the surface, the white limestone shelf was visible, cut by deep, dark fissures where the rock had collapsed into ancient submerged caverns. "The freshwater discharge from the inland aquifers creates a halocline right at the surface. You can see the shimmering layer where the fresh runoff meets the salt water."

Veerle adjusted her headset, watching the hydrophone trace scrolling across a ruggedized tablet screen mounted near the helm. "Background noise is surprisingly high. It's not marine life, and it's not commercial traffic from the Alghero shipping lane. It's low-frequency structural vibration."

"The cave?" Geronimo asked, throttling back as they approached the base of the cliff.

"It’s localized," Veerle replied, zooming in on the real-time fast-Fourier-transform spectrum. "A steady hum at fourteen hertz, with periodic spikes between forty and sixty hertz. It’s coming from inside the cliff base."

Ahead of them, barely visible above the waterline, opened the wide, dark archway of the Grotta di Nettuno. The sea-level entrance was a natural porch carved by wave action into the solid karst. Outside, the turquoise water of the Mediterranean lapped gently against the limestone threshold; inside, the light died rapidly, vanishing into a vast underground chamber that extended deep into the belly of the headland.

Geronimo nudged the *Bisso* forward, bringing her through the mouth of the cavern. The glare of the morning sky vanished instantly, replaced by a cool, damp darkness that smelled intensely of mineral dust, wet stone, and deep-sea brine.

The air temperature dropped five degrees in seconds. Above them, unseen stalactites dripped water into the dark expanse of Lago Lamarmora, one of the largest subterranean saltwater lakes in Europe. The sound of the falling drops—*tik... tik... tik...*—echoed with unsettling precision off unseen stone vaults.

"Engage low-beam forward lights," Geronimo instructed softly.

Veerle flicked a switch. Two narrow beams of warm LED light cut through the gloom, illuminating the pale, towering columns of calcified stone that rose out of the mirror-still lake. In the center of the lake stood the great stalagmite formation known as the *Trono di Nettuno*, its fluted limestone folds resembling the draped robes of a petrified titan.

"Depth inside the lake varies from nine to twelve meters," Katia said, consulting the acoustic chart. "The water is completely still on the surface, but the hydrophone is picking up intense micro-currents near the base of the columns."

"Keep the motor at dead slow," Geronimo said. "We don't want to wake any surface ripples that could distort the laser scan."

The *Bisso* drifted over the black water of Lago Lamarmora. The silence of the cave was absolute, yet underneath the silence, a physical sensation began to press against the crew’s sternums. It was not a sound heard by the ear, but a deep, thrumming resonance that vibrated through the hull of the aluminum boat, through the soles of their boots, and into their bones.

"Do you feel that?" Katia whispered. She pulled off her beanie, her dark hair rising slightly in the static-charged air of the chamber.

"The fourteen-hertz carrier wave," Veerle said, her eyes fixed on her monitor. The signal trace was no longer a flat line punctuated by minor spikes; it had turned into a dense, rhythmic wave form. "It’s intensifying. The source isn't the open water outside. It's coming from the deeper galleries past the Reggia room."

Geronimo steered the launch around the base of a massive mineral column. The passage narrowed, leading toward the upper, dry galleries accessible by narrow stone pathways carved for visitors, but down at water level, the salt lake extended into unmapped karstic siphon tubes that penetrated deep beneath the Capo Caccia peninsula.

Suddenly, the acoustic monitor emitted a harsh, distorted burst of static.

Veerle winced, pulling her headphones away from her ears. "Spike! Massive amplitude jump at sixty hertz!"

Before Geronimo could answer, a sound reverberated through the cavernous vaults of the cave. It did not sound like collapsing rock or breaking waves. It was a dark, metallic, suffocating rhythm—a heavy, rhythmic clanging like massive bronze and iron bells struck in violent, chaotic sequence inside a narrow stone chamber.

*CLANG. THUD. CLANG-CLANG.*

The sound was visceral, heavy, and wet, echoing off the limestone walls with a suffocating density that made the air in the cabin feel thick. It was the distinct, terrifying cadence of the *campanaccu*—the massive iron and bronze cowbells worn on the backs of the masked figures of the inland highlands during the archaic winter rituals.

"That's impossible," Katia said, her voice trembling slightly as she leaned over the acoustic readout. "That’s the cadence of *S'Urtzu*. The heavy, dragging stride... the metallic resonance of iron and bronze clashing against hides."

"It's an acoustic illusion," Veerle said, her fingers flying across the diagnostic keyboard, trying to filter the signal. "It’s a standing wave created by wind pressure forced through the upper blowholes of the karst cliff!"

"No," Katia insisted, pointing to the real-time audio spectrum. "Look at the fundamental frequencies. Wind turbulence creates white noise or broad-spectrum harmonics. This has discrete tonal peaks at 112, 224, and 448 hertz. These are the resonant frequencies of hand-hammered bronze and cast iron. It's the exact acoustic signature of the *tzimborros* worn by the *Urtzu* in the central mountains."

The clanging grew louder, subterranean and rhythmic, accompanied by a sound like heavy, sodden sheepskins being dragged over wet limestone. *SLIP... DRAG... CLANG.* The water of Lago Lamarmora, previously flat as glass, began to form tight, concentric rings around the hull of the *Bisso*.

"The water isn't moving because of wind," Geronimo said, his hand gripping the throttle, his eyes scanning the pitch-black alcoves near the water's edge. "The rock itself is vibrating. The entire karst matrix beneath Capo Caccia is acting as a soundboard."

Over the radio, a burst of heavily distorted voice traffic broke through from Team B.

"...*Bisso*, this is... *Porto di Alghero*..." Maris’s voice was chopped into fragments by electromagnetic interference. "...we are reading... massive seismic micro-tremors... originating... beneath the headland... Do you... copy?"

"Maris, this is Geronimo," he replied, speaking clearly into his lapel mic. "We have high-amplitude acoustic resonance inside the grotto. Rhythmic metallic interference. We are holding position in Lago Lamarmora."

"...get out... the tide..." Maris’s voice was lost in a wall of rhythmic, bell-like static that matched the sound echoing inside the cavern.

"Look at the water level!" Katia pointed down to the base of the *Trono di Nettuno*. 

The water in the lake was dropping—not rapidly, but perceptibly, pulling away from the white mineral rings on the columns at a rate of several centimeters per minute. As the water retreated, it revealed a submerged structural archway at the base of the lake's eastern wall, previously hidden beneath ten meters of dark salt water.

The acoustic resonance seemed to emanate directly from this newly exposed siphon. The metallic clanging reached a deafening crescendo, reverberating through the hollow spaces of the cavern until the air itself seemed to vibrate with the agony of the doomed, thrashing beast of the myth—the *S'Urtzu*, hunted and striking its heavy iron bells against the dark earth before its ritual sacrifice.

"The siphon is draining into a lower chamber," Veerle shouted over the din. "The pressure drop in the inland water table is forcing air through the upper karst vents at supersonic speeds, exciting the natural acoustic resonance of the cavern!"

"It's not just air!" Katia said, her face illuminated by the green glow of the laser scanner. The scanner was mapping the geometry of the newly exposed archway. "Look at the carving on the keystone of that submerged arch!"

Geronimo leaned forward, directing the high-intensity spot beam down toward the exposed stone above the swirling siphon water. 

Carved into the dense limestone, preserved beneath the salt water for centuries, was a deeply incised symbol: three concentric circles intersected by a single vertical line, flanked by stylized representations of horned figures carrying heavy loads on their shoulders.

"The mark of the foundry," Katia breathed, taking rapid high-resolution photos with her damp-proof camera. "The same mark found on the bronze votive figures of the central interior. This cave wasn't just a natural shelter—it was an acoustic amplifier built to transmit subterranean signals across the gulf."

The rhythmic clanging of the *S'Urtzu* resonance gave one final, shattering stroke—a sound so heavy that small fragments of dried calcite drifted down from the cavern ceiling like grey snow. Then, as suddenly as it had begun, the pressure equalization finished. The water level in Lago Lamarmora stabilized, the swirling current in the siphon slowed to a gentle trickle, and the metallic echo faded back into the dark, hollow dripping of the stone.

The silence that returned was absolute, heavier and colder than before.

Geronimo checked the telemetry monitor. The fourteen-hertz hum had dropped back to background levels. On the radio console, the red signal light flickered once, and Maris’s voice came through crisp and clear.

"Team A, this is Alghero base. Signal cleared up. We have secured the yacht at Bastioni Magellano. What is your status?"

Geronimo took a long breath, staring at the wet, newly exposed archway and the carved bronze-age glyphs glistening in the beam of his lantern.

"Alghero base, this is Team A," Geronimo said quietly into the microphone. "We have reached the target area. The karst has opened. Prepare Team B for onshore field deployment. We have found the key to the western signal network."
