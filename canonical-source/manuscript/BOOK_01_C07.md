# Book I Chapter 7: The Weight of the Rope

There was no magic to save them. The Obsidian Eye remained dormant in its case; the Janas Key sat untouched in the fissure. 

Geronimo didn't hesitate. He abandoned the Key, dropping to his stomach on the freezing, razor-sharp limestone. André was already moving, uncoiling the sixty-foot static climbing rope from his pack. He threw a heavy carabiner loop toward the churning water. 

The fisherman caught it blindly. The instant the line went taut, the ocean's dead weight hit.

Geronimo and André slammed against the rock, their boots scrabbling for purchase. The rope burned through Geronimo's gloves, tearing the skin from his palms. The pain was absolute, a blinding white flare in his forearms. 

*La paradura.* The ancient Sardinian code of mutual aid. You do not leave a man to the elements.

Katia threw her weight onto Geronimo's legs, anchoring him. Veerle and Inga grabbed the slack behind André. For twenty agonizing minutes, it was a pure, brutal physical struggle against the Mediterranean. Muscle, grit, and the screaming of the wind. 

Inch by inch, they hauled the man out of the impact zone, dragging his battered body onto the lower landing. Geronimo collapsed, his hands bloodied, his core temperature plummeting toward hypothermia. 

Above them, the wail of sirens cut through the Mistral. The Coast Guard had been called by tourists on the cliff top. The group looked at each other in dawning horror. They had saved a life, but they were now trapped in a highly visible, bureaucratic rescue operation. The anonymity they had fought so hard to protect was gone. And below, in the receding tide, the Janas Key washed silently into the deep water.
