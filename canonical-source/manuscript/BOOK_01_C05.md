# Book I Chapter 5: The Red Ochre Boundary

The November sun broke over the limestone plains of the Nurra, casting long, sharp shadows across the Anghelu Ruju necropolis. The air smelled of wet thyme and the metallic tang of yesterday's storm. The *domus de janas*—the houses of the fairies, the tombs of the dead—pockmarked the earth like entrances to a submerged world. 

Geronimo carried the waterproof case. Inside, the Obsidian Eye was heavy, inert. They had arrived as tourists. Ordinary. Legal. Katia's wrist was wrapped tight, throbbing in the cold, but she walked with the disciplined, even gait of a scholar. Veerle knelt by Tomb 28, tracing the air above the carved bull horns, refusing to touch the red ochre that still stained the rock after five thousand years. 

"Blood and rebirth," Veerle whispered. Her breath plumed in the freezing morning air. "They didn't just bury their dead here. They aligned them."

"Hold the lens," Maris said, her eyes scanning the perimeter. The hired van sat legally in the gravel visitor lot, a lifeline they couldn't afford to lose.

Geronimo lifted the Obsidian Eye from its case. He didn't look through it—he let the morning sun hit the curved black glass. The light refracted, projecting a thin, crimson beam that hit the red ochre of the doorway, bouncing perfectly westward. It pointed like a spear toward the sheer, violent cliffs of Capo Caccia in the distance. The architecture wasn't just a tomb; it was a map aligned with the equinox.

"Got it," André muttered, logging the trajectory into a localized offline map. "We move."

But the hazard didn't come from the magic. It came from the mundane.

"Three o'clock," Maris said, her voice dropping to a terrifying calm. "German tour group. Third man on the left. Blue windbreaker."

Geronimo didn't turn his head. He used the reflection of the Eye's casing. The man wasn't looking at the tombs. His eyes were scanning the crowd, fingers resting on his collar. A Sentinel spotter. Charcoal-coated comms hidden in plain sight. 

They were in a public, regulated archaeological park. A firefight here meant total exposure, arrest, and the end of the journey. 

"Disperse," Katia ordered softly, overriding her pain. "Merge with the crowd. Do not run. Meet at the van in three minutes."

The adrenaline spiked, cold and sharp. They slipped into the labyrinth of tourists and ancient stone, walking the tightrope between the mundane world and the Father's encroaching shadow. Geronimo fell in step behind a German family, keeping his shoulders loose, his hand near the waterproof case. The spotter turned, eyes sweeping over the limestone, missing Geronimo by a fraction of an inch. 

They reached the van. The doors slid shut. Anonymity was broken, but they were alive. Capo Caccia awaited.
