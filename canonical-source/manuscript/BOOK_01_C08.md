# Book I Chapter 8: The Photograph at the Harbour Wall

They were wrapped in foil thermal blankets, sitting on the stone benches of the Alghero Catalan bastions. The Coast Guard had taken their statements, recorded their passport numbers, and commended their bravery. 

Geronimo's hands were wrapped in thick white gauze. The pain was a dull, constant throb, grounding him in the immediate reality of their failure. The Janas Key was gone. 

Veerle sat next to him, staring at her encrypted tablet. "It's already online," she said quietly.

She turned the screen. It was a digital news article from a local Sardinian outlet: *Tourist Rescue at Capo Caccia*. The photograph showed Geronimo and André being led up the steps by paramedics. 

"Look at the background," Veerle whispered. She pinched and zoomed the image. 

Standing on the upper viewing deck, blurred but unmistakable, was the man in the blue windbreaker from Anghelu Ruju. The Sentinel spotter. He was looking directly at the camera. 

"They tracked the rescue choppers," Katia said, her voice hollow. 

"The rented van is compromised," Maris stated, her mind instantly shifting to tactical logistics. "They'll have the plates. If we go back to the marina for the *Sentina*, they'll box us in on the water."

"Then how do we move?" André asked, his face tight with exhaustion.

Maris pulled up a topographical map. "We disappear into the infrastructure. The Sardinian rail backbone. We walk to the Alghero station, buy ordinary paper tickets with cash, and take the narrow-gauge train to Sassari. From there, the main line south to Macomer. We become ghosts in the transit system."
