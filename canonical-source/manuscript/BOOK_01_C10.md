# Book I Chapter 10: The Nuragic Interior

They abandoned the rail network, trading Katia's emergency cash reserves for a battered, decades-old Fiat Panda from a local mechanic who asked no questions. The drive east into the Barbagia was treacherous, the roads winding precariously along deep gorges and ancient forests.

This was the true heart of Sardinia. The land of vendettas and silence, where the Roman legions had never truly conquered. 

Inga, usually quiet, leaned forward from the backseat. "The electromagnetic readings," she said, tapping her modified geiger counter. "They're spiking. It's not radiation. It's terrestrial magnetism. The Nuragic towers—the *nuraghi*—they aren't just watchtowers. They're nodes."

They camped that night near the ruins of a sprawling nuragic complex. The air was freezing, carrying the scent of wild thyme and woodsmoke. Geronimo held the Obsidian Eye to the starlight. The stone seemed to drink the darkness, glowing with a faint, internal crimson light that mapped perfectly to the constellations above the Barbagia. The ancient Sardinians hadn't just built towers; they had built a terrestrial reflection of the sky.
