# Book I Chapter 6: The Roe Deer's Steps

The Mistral hit them the moment they crested the Capo Caccia promontory. It wasn't just wind; it was a physical force, a freezing, salt-heavy wall of air blasting off the churning Mediterranean. Below them lay the *Escala del Cabirol*—the Roe Deer's Steps. Six hundred and fifty-four stairs carved directly into the sheer limestone cliff face, plunging toward the violent sea.

"Keep your center of gravity low!" André shouted over the roar of the wind, his voice barely carrying two feet. 

The descent was brutal. Katia's bad wrist was useless on the handrails; she relied entirely on her legs, her breath tearing from her throat in ragged gasps. Geronimo took the lead, the waterproof case strapped tight against his chest. The magic of the Eye was silent here, dwarfed by the sheer scale of the Nurra coast's historical reality—a natural barrier that had broken ships and men for millennia.

They reached the lower landings where the sea spray felt like flying glass. There, wedged in a high-tide fissure just above the crash zone, it rested: the Janas Key. A jagged construct of oxidized bronze and raw rock-crystal. 

Geronimo reached for it. 

The sea drew back, an unnatural, sudden silence falling over the ledge. 

"Rogue wave!" Maris screamed from the upper landing.

It didn't hit them. It hit the limestone shelf thirty yards to their left. A local rock-fisher, a man in a yellow slicker who had ventured too far out, was picked up like a child's toy. The white water swallowed him, instantly dragging him toward the deadly undertow of the cliff base.
