# Book I: The Obsidian Eye
## Chapter 3 — The Titan's Wake

The Mediterranean Sea was not a body of water; tonight, it was a predator.

The *Cruise Roma*, a fifty-thousand-ton Grimaldi passenger ferry, shuddered as it absorbed another broadside impact from the squall. Steel groaned. Deep in the bowels of the ship, the massive diesel engines hammered out a rhythmic, vibrating baseline that vibrated through the soles of Geronimo’s boots. 

They were trapped in the aft passenger lounge on Deck 6. The air was a suffocating cocktail of diesel exhaust, stale coffee, and the sharp, acidic tang of seasickness from the hundreds of desperate passengers who hadn't secured cabins. People were sprawled across the faux-leather benches, clutching plastic bags and praying in a dozen languages.

Geronimo sat rigidly on the floor in the corner, his back pressed against the vibrating bulkhead. His hand hovered over the breast pocket of his jacket, feeling the hard, rectangular weight of the Obsidian Eye. 

"Stop touching it," Katia whispered. She sat beside him, her knees pulled to her chest, her dark eyes darting around the dim lounge. She looked exhausted, her raincoat still damp, but her gaze was razor-sharp. 

"If I don't feel it, I think I'll wake up," Geronimo rasped. "I keep thinking I'm back in Barcelona, designing the lobby for that tech firm, and this is just a fever."

Maris appeared from the crowd, carrying three lukewarm bottles of water. He handed them out in silence. The big man was operating purely on logistical autopilot. Behind him, Veerle and Inga sat back-to-back on a narrow bench, sleeping in five-minute tactical bursts. 

André was pacing. He had been pacing since they left the port. 

"This doesn't make sense," André said, stopping in front of Geronimo. His hands were shaking. "A pressure anomaly. A freak storm. And some thugs trying to break into an apartment. That's what happened. Gravity doesn't reverse, Geronimo. Men don't walk without casting shadows."

"You saw the door splinter," Geronimo said, his voice flat. "You saw the water crawling *up* the glass."

"I saw an old building fail in a hurricane!" André hissed, his voice cracking. "I am a structural engineer! I deal in physics! What you're talking about is—"

The ferry dropped. 

It didn't just roll; the bow plunged into a massive trough, sending the ship into a violent freefall. A collective scream tore through the passenger lounge as bags, coffee cups, and unanchored chairs flew across the room. 

Geronimo slammed his forearm into the bulkhead to brace himself. Katia crashed into his shoulder. 

And then, the lights went out. 

The ensuing darkness was absolute, accompanied by the terrifying silence of the ventilation system dying. A second later, the emergency backup kicked in—blood-red halogens spaced every twenty meters along the ceiling. They cast long, demonic shadows across the panicked passengers.

"The engines are still running," Maris shouted over the rising din of the crowd. "Just a localized breaker trip!"

Geronimo’s heart spiked. "The dogs. Mia and Tina."

"They're on Deck 7 in the kennels," Inga said, snapping instantly awake, her hand instinctively going to her belt where she usually kept a knife she didn't have. "If the power is cut to that deck, the climate control fails. It’s exposed to the outer hull. They'll freeze, or panic and hurt themselves."

"I'll go," Geronimo said, pushing himself off the floor. 

"I'm coming with you," André said, grabbing a heavy metal Maglite from Maris’s go-bag. "I need to do something physical or I'm going to lose my mind."

"Keep your heads down," Veerle warned, her pale eyes catching the red emergency lights. "If whoever was chasing us had a network, they might have people on this boat."

Geronimo and André pushed their way through the screaming, disoriented crowd, reaching the aft stairwell. 

The descent to Deck 7 was a plunge into the ship's industrial underbelly. The air grew immediately colder, stripping the warmth from their lungs. The red emergency lights flickered unreliably here, casting stroboscopic flashes down the long, empty promenade corridors that ran parallel to the outer hull. 

"This is insane," André muttered, his breath pluming in the cold air as they jogged down the corridor. "We are adults running from a fairy tale."

"Focus on the dogs," Geronimo said. 

They passed the grand promenade—a section of the ship lined with floor-to-ceiling decorative mirrors intended to make the narrow hallway feel spacious. 

The ship pitched violently to the port side. 

Geronimo stumbled, catching himself on the brass handrail. André stumbled with him. 

But as Geronimo looked into the massive mirror panel beside them, his blood turned to ice. 

In the reflection, the corridor was entirely empty. 

No, not empty. Geronimo stared harder. His and André's reflections were there, but they were doing something impossible. While the real Geronimo and André had stumbled to the *left* to compensate for the ship's roll, their reflections in the mirror were leaning to the *right*. 

They were out of sync. 

"André," Geronimo whispered, his vocal cords paralyzed. 

André looked at the mirror. The engineer’s face went completely slack. The Maglite slipped from his fingers, hitting the carpeted floor with a dull thud. 

In the mirror, their reflections slowly stopped leaning. The reflection of Geronimo turned its head, looked directly at the real André, and smiled. It was a smile of pure, ancient malice. 

*Crack.* 

A massive web of fractures exploded across the mirror's surface, distorting the grinning reflection into a thousand jagged shards. 

"Physics, André," Geronimo breathed, grabbing the terrified man by the collar and hauling him forward. "Forget physics. Move!"

They sprinted down the remaining corridor, bursting through the heavy fire doors into the Deck 7 Kennel Bay. 

The noise was deafening. Dozens of dogs in steel cages were barking, howling, and throwing themselves against the mesh wire in a frenzy of absolute terror. 

Mia and Tina were in a large double crate near the rear maintenance door. They weren't barking. They were backed into the furthest corner of the cage, teeth bared, snarling with a low, guttural intensity directed squarely at the maintenance door.

The heavy steel door, which led directly to the outer weather deck, was vibrating. 

Standing in front of the door was a man. 

He wore a grease-stained mechanic’s jumpsuit. He looked utterly ordinary—until he turned his head. His eyes were entirely black. No sclera, no iris, just twin voids of obsidian that seemed to pull the red emergency light into them. 

The man was holding the massive hydraulic lever that sealed the outer door. The storm outside was raging. If he opened that door, the sea would flood the compartment, drowning every animal in the room and instantly compromising the ferry’s structural integrity.

"He is coming for the Map," the man said. 

The voice did not come from his throat. It sounded like grinding stones, spoken in a guttural, ancient dialect of Logudorese that Geronimo understood without knowing how. *The blood memory.*

"Get away from the door!" André screamed, his terror transmuting into desperate rage. He scooped up a heavy red fire extinguisher from the wall bracket. 

The possessed mechanic didn't blink. He simply gripped the hydraulic lever and pulled downward. 

The pressure seal hissed. Icy seawater immediately sprayed into the kennel bay, accompanied by the deafening roar of the Mediterranean squall. The ship lurched, destabilized by the sudden breach. 

"No!" Geronimo roared. He launched himself across the wet metal grating, tackling the mechanic. 

Hitting the man felt like hitting a concrete pillar. The mechanic didn't flinch. He backhanded Geronimo with a terrifying, casual strength, sending him crashing into the steel cages. 

André charged, swinging the heavy metal fire extinguisher like a battering ram. He slammed it directly into the mechanic’s ribcage. The impact was sickeningly loud—bones definitely shattered—but the mechanic merely tilted his head, grabbed the fire extinguisher, and ripped it out of André's hands, tossing the engineer aside like a ragdoll.

The outer door was opening wider. The water was up to their ankles, freezing and black. 

Geronimo scrambled to his feet. His chest was screaming in pain. He didn't have a weapon. He didn't have a plan. He only had the Book. 

He ripped the Obsidian Eye from his jacket. 

As the mechanic reached for the hydraulic lever to rip the door completely open, Geronimo lunged, driving the leather-bound book directly into the man's face, pressing the black-stone seal against his forehead.

The reaction was instantaneous and apocalyptic. 

A sound like a thunderclap detonated in the small room. The mechanic’s back arched violently. Black, oily smoke poured from his mouth and eyes, hissing as it hit the freezing seawater. 

The man collapsed, completely devoid of whatever force had been puppeteering him, splashing lifelessly into the flooded deck. 

Geronimo didn't hesitate. He grabbed the hydraulic lever, straining with every ounce of adrenaline left in his system, and hauled it upward. The heavy steel door ground shut, the pressure seal locking with a heavy, final *thunk*. 

The roar of the ocean was silenced. 

Geronimo collapsed against the door, coughing up seawater, his hands clutching the book to his chest. 

André crawled over the grating, his face bruised, his clothes soaked. He looked at the unconscious mechanic, then at the book in Geronimo’s arms. 

"I'm sorry," André whispered, his voice trembling. "I'm so sorry I doubted you."

Geronimo closed his eyes. "We're alive. Just get the dogs."

***

Dawn broke over the Mediterranean not with sunlight, but with a bruised, violent purple that slowly bled through the storm clouds. 

The *Cruise Roma* limped into the Gulf of Asinara, its engines sounding ragged and exhausted. 

Geronimo stood on the upper weather deck, his hands gripping the salt-crusted railing. The wind was still fierce, but the rain had stopped. Katia stood beside him, her shoulder pressed against his. 

Through the morning mist, a towering, jagged silhouette tore through the horizon. 

Sardinia. 

It did not look like the pristine holiday destination from the travel brochures. It looked like a fortress of dark granite, rising from the churning sea like the spine of a sleeping titan. The sheer cliffs of the Asinara island off the coast looked brutal and unyielding. 

Even from the deck of the ferry, miles out, the wind carried a scent that hit Geronimo with the force of a physical memory. It was the sharp, resinous smell of wild myrtle—*mirto*—mixed with sea salt, crushed juniper, and something ancient and metallic. 

It was the smell of the bookshop. It was the smell of his mother’s coat. 

"It’s huge," Katia whispered, staring at the sheer scale of the coastline. 

"It's a labyrinth," Geronimo said, his voice quiet but hard. The events of the night had burned the last of his hesitation away. He was no longer an architect fleeing a mystery. He was a target entering a war zone. 

Behind them, Maris, Veerle, Inga, and André stepped onto the deck, Mia and Tina walking closely at their heels. They were battered, soaked, and exhausted, but their eyes were clear. The skepticism was gone. The cell was forged. 

The ferry’s horn blasted, a deep, mournful sound that echoed off the Sardinian cliffs. 

Geronimo looked down at the water crashing against the hull. The Hounds had found them on the water. Now, they were entering the Father's domain. 

The crossing was over. The hunt had begun.
